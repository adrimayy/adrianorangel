Columbia extension

My EHHS extension rebuilt for college: a browser home screen for the Columbia sites I open every day, organized around my own class schedule. It runs in both Chrome and Firefox from the same folder. Source on GitHub.

Why

A college week has no bell schedule: every day is different, with long gaps between classes in different buildings. I wanted the toolbar to know what I have next and get me to that class's materials in one click. Anything I only open once a semester stays a bookmark.

How it works

  • Now / Next. The popup opens with the class I'm in or the one coming up: its room, time left and that class's own links, such as the course site during Advanced Programming or Gradescope during Econ.
  • A timer on the icon. It counts down the class I'm in, and the hour before the next one. It's quiet on weekends, breaks and study days, and shows finals and one-off sessions such as a make-up lab.
  • A schedule page. Today's classes with a progress bar and the free gaps between them, upcoming exams, and the week at a glance.
  • One file per semester. Classes, rooms, the term's dates and breaks all live in one data file, so a new semester means editing that file and nothing else.

What I learned

  • Most of a good rebuild is deciding what to keep. The EHHS extension's home screen, editing page and link syncing carried over as they were. The bell schedules, snow-day announcements and 117-link catalog came out, replaced by a weekly class schedule and about 30 links I actually use.
  • One extension, two browsers. Chrome runs extension background code as a service worker, and Firefox runs it as an event page. Listing both in the manifest, and loading files only where the service-worker way exists, lets the same folder pass Mozilla's validator and run in Chrome.
