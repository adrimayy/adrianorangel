Vocab ladders

Two Anki decks, built in Python, that start at the edge of what I already know and climb from there: English Vocab Ladder (CS) and Spanish Vocab Ladder (MX).

Why

I grew up speaking Spanish at home, and my vocabulary has gaps in both languages. Generic word lists waste time on words I know, so each deck starts with a placement quiz.

How they're made

  • Placement quiz. Up to three adaptive rounds sample each level, with a "Not sure" option so guessing doesn't inflate the result. The English deck placed me around the 5,000–7,000 most common word families.
  • Leveled by real standards. English levels follow frequency bands (Nation's bands, Coxhead's Academic Word List), computed with the wordfreq package instead of guessed. Spanish follows CEFR and the Instituto Cervantes, plus sections for Mexican, NYC Caribbean and Tampico slang.
  • Card design from memory research. One fact per card. Two directions per word: recognize it in a sentence, then type it from its meaning. A draining timer pushes real recall, and only one element per card is highlighted. I skipped "memory fonts" because the research behind them doesn't replicate.
  • Build and check. The decks are generated with genanki, with stable IDs so a rebuilt deck keeps my review progress. Before each release, cards are rendered at phone width in Playwright, day and night, and checked so every answer makes sense on its own.

The whole process is written down as a reusable spec, so a new subject gets the same quiz, leveling and checks.
